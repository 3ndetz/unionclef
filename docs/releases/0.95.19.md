# 0.95.19 — stand-finding stops rejecting a stand because the body is in the way (G108 portal)

Follow-up to 0.95.18. Fixes the last standing full-flood failure at its root: the build drain
pillaring a ground cell into its own body and jamming for ever.

## The failure: a bottom-row cell placed by pillaring INTO itself

Measured on the full obsidian flood (baseline ~4/6 on a healthy stand, worse when the stand is
loaded), the intermittent failure was always the same shape: the build stalled on a bottom-row frame
cell (e.g. 2358,-57,357), the body standing in or beside that cell, "Wandering" for the rest of the
window (measured 246 s on one run) and never placing it. The drain's own trace named it:

```
walk='[1 pillarbase=2358,-57,357 from=2357,-57,357] [2 pillarbase=... from=2359,-57,357] PILLAR(...)'
blockedByOwnBody=5..10
```

The drain was PILLARING a ground cell — walking the body INTO the cell to jump-place — instead of
placing it from the side. The body then stood in the very cell it had to fill, so the placement was
refused (the block would intersect the player), `blockedByOwnBody` climbed, and it never recovered.

## The root: `placementStand` gated stand-finding on the body's current position

`BlockPlaceHelper.placementStand` answers "WHERE do I stand to place this cell". It also required
`placementPlausible(target)` for each candidate facing — a port of upstream's
`BuilderProcess.placementGoal` (BuilderProcess.java:1058). But `placementPlausible` tests whether the
block would intersect an ENTITY, and the only entity that matters is the player. Testing that against
where the body is RIGHT NOW is the wrong question for a method whose whole job is to find a position
the body will MOVE to. When the body had drifted onto the target cell — routine for a bottom-row cell
reached from the gather side, or after a wander lands the body on it — plausibility failed for every
facing, `placementStand` returned null, and `drainQueue` fell to its PILLAR branch
(`sideStand == null && standable(head)`). Pillaring walks the body further into the cell, so
plausibility keeps failing: a self-sustaining jam.

The clean-state world confirmed the stand was always there: with the body elsewhere, the east and
west stands (2359,-57,357 / 2357,-57,357) are air over stone pad — perfectly standable. Only the
body being in the cell hid them.

## The fix (core, one line of logic)

`placementStand` no longer gates on `placementPlausible`. Stand-finding is about a FUTURE position, so
the body's current overlap is irrelevant. The real placement-plausibility check already runs — and
stays — in `drainQueue`'s main place loop, at the moment of placing, when the body is actually at the
stand (`blockedByOwnBody++` there). `adjacentStand` already excludes the target cell and any stand
whose head is the target, so the chosen stand never leaves the body in the cell it fills. Net effect:
when the body is on the target cell, `placementStand` now returns the real side stand, the drain walks
the body OUT to it, and the place succeeds — instead of pillaring into itself.

Column-top cells that genuinely need pillaring are unaffected: their sides are open air, so
`adjacentStand` still finds no stand and the pillar branch still fires for them.

## What was tried and reverted

Turning on the three wander-recovery flags (`wanderSearchMustMove`, `wanderTargetFollowsTheGround`,
`wanderSpiralCountsLegsNotTries`) to make the escape wander re-pick faster. Measured neutral-to-worse
(3/6 on a contended stand) and, decisively, the body then thrashed near the frame but the place still
never landed — proving the wander was a SYMPTOM and the place-stall the root. Reverted. Also removed
the earlier dead directed-escape experiment from `ConstructNetherPortalObsidianTask` (it never fired).

## Results

Full obsidian flood (`OBS=1`, 6 runs each, fresh client, uncontended stand):

| build | flood pass rate |
|---|---|
| 0.95.18 baseline | 4/6 |
| 0.95.19 (this fix) | 6/6 |

The two failures the baseline shows are both the bottom-row cell jam described above. With the fix
the drain walks the body out to the side stand and places, and all six runs built + lit the portal
(three of them teleported the bot into the nether, the strongest proof). A prior fix-arm reading of
3/6 was discarded as confounded: it ran with a heavy per-tick block-probe instrument (about a dozen
`docker exec` reads every wander tick) that starved the client during the exact phase under test;
removing the probe gave the clean 6/6.

## Regression

`placementStand` is called only from the build-queue drain, so the nav suite's place/pillar/bridge
courses are the guard:

```
nav_break  nav_wall2  nav_bridge  nav_flat  nav_staircase  nav_descend    6/6 PASS
```

nav_break (mine then continue), nav_wall2 (pillar) and nav_bridge (bridge placement) all still pass,
so the drain change does not regress movement-time building.

## How to test

```
OBS=1 python3 deploy/runner/nether_portal_test.py   # full flood -> gather -> build -> light
```

PASS = a real `minecraft:nether_portal` at the build, or the bot teleporting to the nether.
