## A zombie was being fought as if its arm were as long as ours

`STRIKE_DISTANCE` is 2.4 and `TOO_CLOSE_DISTANCE` is 1.6. Both were derived for a duel — an opponent
who reaches as far as we do, 3.0. A zombie's arm is **2.25** by the same eye-to-hitbox measure, so
those two numbers park the bot **inside** the arm and do not drift back out until 1.6, which is
deeper in. Every exchange conceded a free swing by construction.

`MOB_STRIKE_DISTANCE` (2.9, outside the arm) and `MOB_BACK_OFF_DISTANCE` (2.65) were derived for
exactly this case — and never read. They were two of **eleven** mob-combat constants that appear
exactly once each in the whole repository: on their own declarations. The mob combat policy they
describe did not exist; a zombie was fought with the player duelling code.

This release wires two of them.

**Twelve runs an arm**, same stand, same day, exact `dmgTaken`:

| band | damage | mean | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.4 (the old default) | 3, 9, 6, 20, 17, 9, 9, 9, 6, 3, 17, 15 | **10.25** | 9.0 |
| 2.9 (the mob band) | 3, 9, 3, 3, 6, 12, 6, 3, 17, 6, **0**, 3 | **5.92** | 4.5 |

Damage nearly halves — 2.0 σ — and that `0` is **`mob_trio`'s first ever pass**. Baselines held
throughout: `mob_melee` 2/2, `mob_weapon_swap` 2/2.

### It overturns a measurement recorded in the same file

A table in `CombatController` read 2.9 as *worse* than 2.4 against three zombies — `12 / 20 / 6`
against `3 / 3 / 9` — and concluded "distance tuning is exhausted, and the default wins". That was
**n=3 an arm** on a course whose spread on one build is 3 to 20. It could not have separated them.
Corrected at the site; the table's 1.8 row and its reasoning about knockback still stand.

### Why this took eight failed hypotheses to find

`mob_trio` gates on zero damage and had never passed. Its own header records eight combat hypotheses
that died there, all judged on `min_hp`, all returning "noise". They were tuning a policy that does
not exist.

Two things made it visible. No mob course had ever printed `gateStats`, and `armHold`/`crowdEsc`/
`crowdPlan` were never exposed over py4j at all — both are now read. And the course cannot resolve
small effects at small n: exact `dmgTaken` ranged 3 to 20 on one build in one day, so every n=2 or
n=3 comparison — including two of mine this session, both reverted — was inside the noise by a
factor of five. The criterion now carries that number and the instruction to budget n≥6.

**Still red**, and honestly so: one pass in twelve is a distribution that has moved, not a course
that works. The remaining nine constants — the press distances, the mob speed model, the swing
cooldown clock and the crowd plan with its horizon, replan interval and opportunity weight — are
still unwired, and their javadoc is left in place as the specification for whoever builds them.
