# 0.95.0 — a survival start to iron tools, recorded

Snapshot of everything since 0.94.0 (2026-09-09 → 2026-09-16): the tool-equip and mining-aim
fixes, water climb-out, and the stall work of rounds 42–49. Verified on the survival stand with
a recorded ten-minute playthrough and on the flat stand with the nav regression suite. Below:
what the bot does now, what changed, where it still stops, and — for each thing — how to see it.

`v0.94.0` stays on GitHub as the fallback.

---

## What it does now

Started fresh in a survival world (`@gamer`, nothing in the pack), in one ten-minute run on the
stand (2026-09-16, `deploy/runner/gamer_smoke.py`, 97 items, 0 deaths):

| Rung | Reached at |
| --- | --- |
| first craft, wooden tools | 42.8 s |
| crafting table, stone tools | 64.4 s |
| furnace, coal | 173.0 s |
| more wood | 194.6 s |
| food (chicken, cooked in a smoker) | 216.4 s |
| iron ore | 367.9 s |
| iron ingots | 389.4 s |
| **iron tools** | **411.2 s** |

On the way it punched trees, placed and used a table, dug into stone with the right tool in
hand, found coal and iron by sight and by digging, pillared out of pits, crossed water and
climbed out onto the far bank, killed and cooked a chicken, and smelted at a furnace it had
placed. No deaths in that run; the earlier rounds (42–49, see `docs/STATUS-2026-09-14.md`) went
from "eight minutes standing at a slot" to the same ladder with 96 items and 0 deaths.

**HOW TO TEST:** on the survival stand (`docker compose -f deploy/compose.test.yml up -d
uctest-gamer-server uctest-mc-tester1`), `python deploy/runner/gamer_smoke.py 1`. It prints the
ladder with timings, deaths, and writes a stall dump to `deploy/runner/freezes/` if no new rung
appears for 150 s. PASS means the run finished without a death. In-game: `@gamer` on any
survival world.

## What changed since 0.94.0

### Mining and the camera

- **One aimer per tick.** Two mining drivers (tungsten's route dig and altoclef's block miner)
  could both steer the camera in the same tick. Vanilla resets break progress every time the
  crosshair leaves a block, so the bot stood with a tool raised, breaking neither — eight
  seconds at 00:12 of the 2026-09-16 recording, `breakMissWhy transit=1035`. The route dig now
  yields while the miner owns the aim (`execDigYieldMiner`), same as the walk path already did.
  Bench: transit misses 77 → 0, both blocks broken in 6 s.
  **HOW TO TEST:** on the flat stand, `mineBlocks([[x,y,z]])` on one block and
  `destroyBlockAt(x+1,y,z)` on its neighbour from py4j; both break, `breakMissWhy` stays 0.
- **Bare-hand mining with a pickaxe in the pack** ("Found better tool, equipping" spam while
  punching stone). The tool-equip and the miner fought over the hand every tick. Fixed at the
  swap; `dbToolEquipped` counts the swaps that stick.
- **The miner swings before the crosshair is on the block** (G50), **mines cells it cannot see**
  (G31), and **the attack key released every tick** (G33) — each fixed at the site.

### Navigation (tungsten)

- **Water: cross, and climb OUT.** The bot swam across fine and then bobbed at the far bank for
  half a minute looking down into the wall. A swim stroke now aims level at the destination
  column (G64), and leaving the water onto a raised bank is its own task (`SwimOutTask`) instead
  of a pillar built in the lake.
  **HOW TO TEST:** `python deploy/runner/run_suite.py nav --only nav_water`; on the survival
  stand any lake between the bot and its goal.
- **Towers.** A tower is built from the base of its cell, a carpet under the feet is cleared
  first (G82); the column the plan chose, not where the body stopped (G51); refused under a
  ceiling the plan meant to mine (G56); a vine in the column no longer turns the jump into a
  climb (G66). **HOW TO TEST:** `pit_escape_test.py`, `carpet_tower_test.py`.
- **The physics hand-off starts from a body at rest (G91).** The physics search copied the
  body's velocity into its root at the moment it was asked, and the replay began when the
  search returned — half a second later, from a body that had already coasted to a stop. The
  replay then ran a little slower than the simulation and missed a lip the plan cleared by
  2.4 cm: `nav_steep` fell into the first gap on every first attempt (0 of 5). The navigator now
  releases every movement key and waits for the body to stop before it asks; first attempts
  5 of 6 clean at 9 s. Same-day playthrough logs show 53 replay aborts in ten minutes of the
  same class from other callers — `pfRootMoving` counts them for the next pass.
  **HOW TO TEST:** `python deploy/runner/run_suite.py nav --only nav_steep --record`; A/B with
  `--pin physicsHandoffFromRest=false`.
- **Routes and hand-offs.** A route no longer outlives the drive that owned it (G52); a
  one-cell incomplete result is fed to the incomplete-route handler instead of being
  discarded (nav_steep's lip); the physics salvage no longer NPEs on an empty guide (G87);
  a goal far below is priced as the dig it is (G73), and a drop more than a fall below is
  reached by digging (G72).
- **Containers and tables.** A container is reached from a cell beside it (G76); "place a
  table nearby" no longer picks the cell the bot stands in (G27); a table is worth four planks,
  not a climb (G80).

### Survival behaviour (altoclef)

- **The slot muzzle** (G83): one click judged by every server packet within 600 ms escalated to
  a 600-second ban on the slot holding the log the plank craft needed. One verdict per click,
  the ladder decays, a muzzle dies with the body that earned it. Round 45: 16 false cancels
  discarded, 1 real muzzle, against 143 in round 42.
- **Drops.** A settled drop that settles again is a new place (G88); the pickaxe fetched for a
  drop is never made from that drop (G89); a drop on a ledge is no longer chased through the
  physics engine for 200 s (G39); pursuit has a budget (G23, partial — see below).
- **Mobs.** Killed by a creeper it was pursuing (G43) → creepers are avoided whether or not they
  see the body (G70) and routes keep clear of them (G75); the hand is taken for a hit that can
  land (G71); "dangerous" means a cave, not a height (G78).
- **Unstuck** was a random dig with the attack key held (G26) and a cooldown that overflowed to
  −268435456 s (G85); both fixed.

The full list is G25–G89 in `docs/BARITONE-GAPS.md`, each with the recording it was seen on,
the cause, and the bench that reproduces it.

### Tooling for the next person

- py4j/MCP: `destroyBlockAt(x,y,z)` runs altoclef's block miner on one block (the "Block in
  range" driver), next to the existing `mineBlocks` (tungsten's executor queue). Together they
  reproduce any two-driver mining fight on the flat stand in one call each.
- Counters in `placeStats`: `breakMissWhy=occluded/transit`, `execDigYieldMiner`,
  `dbAimWait`, `breakAim`, `dbBlocked=selfFloor/unclearable/noReach`, `shBan`, `dbToolEquipped`,
  `navHandoffRest=settled/timedOut`, `pfRootMoving`.
- A replay that diverges from its simulation can be read tick by tick:
  `run_suite.py nav --only <course> --pin verboseDebugLogging=true` makes `Agent.compare` print
  every real-vs-sim mismatch (position, velocity, sprint, jump cooldown, collisions) to the
  client log. That trace is what found G91.
- `deploy/runner/gamer_smoke.py N` — the fresh-start playthrough with the item ladder and the
  stall dump; `tg_clip.py` / `tg_speedup.py` — a clip of a recording, sped up, for the operator.
- `docs/CHECKLIST.md` §4y — how an autonomous run is reviewed: frames at every multi-second
  hold, two task lines on screen means two drivers, the counters that name a mechanism, and
  reproducing the mechanism on the flat stand when the scene will not replay.

## Where it stops now

- **After iron tools the bot wanders.** In the recorded run it walked 60 blocks for drops, came
  back to a smoker it had placed 11 blocks underground, and spent the last three minutes
  digging back down to it. Nothing is stuck — every route ends — but the workstations are left
  where they were first placed and the bot returns to them from wherever it is. The return
  route in that run was stopped from outside (a task switch), not by the terrain; the
  write-up is in `TODOS.md`.
- **No bed, no shelter.** Nights are spent working. With hostiles at spawn the run can end in a
  death (round 43: two minutes at 1.9 hp mining, killed by a spider). The night track (G77)
  is deliberately not started: it does not block the ladder.
- **Drop pursuit on hills** (G23) has a budget now but not a route: a drop on a snowy slope can
  still cost a minute.
- **Planner node rate** (5–7k nodes per 250 ms budget) is the ceiling for long digs; a five-block
  dig plan sometimes comes out partial and the vein is worked one block at a time.
- The nav regression suite on this build: **14 of 14** (`python deploy/runner/run_suite.py
  nav`, flat, staircase, steep, gaps, descend, cliff, water, ladder, slime, break, wall2,
  bridge, hazard, notch; 29–30 fps). `nav_gaps` keeps its known one-in-a-few fall into the void
  on the first go (a course-intrinsic flake recorded in `scenarios_nav.py`) and passes on the
  retry; `nav_steep` has one remaining first-attempt fall with a different signature (G91b in
  `TODOS.md`), one in six runs.

## The road ahead

Iron tools are the first rung that needs everything at once: wood, stone, fuel, food, ore,
smelting, and the walking between them. The next rungs reuse those pieces:

1. **Shield and iron armour** — crafting only; the recipes are already in the ladder.
2. **Diamonds** — a dig to y=−58 with the tool in hand, lava handled by the water-bucket chain,
   the vein worked as one target (the partial-plan item above is the blocker).
3. **The nether** — obsidian (a bucket cast, or a diamond pickaxe), the portal, blaze rods,
   ender pearls; the `@gamer` task already knows the sequence, none of it has been exercised
   on the stand since the tungsten migration.
4. **Stronghold and the End** — eyes of ender, the portal room, the dragon.

Alongside: workstations that follow the work (carry the table and furnace instead of walking
back), the night track once the ladder is long enough to need it, and the planner's node rate.
