## The builder is gone, and it was never building anything

`PlaceBlockTask` handed baritone's `BuilderProcess` a schematic that was one block wide, one tall
and one deep. "Put one of these blocks in that cell", dressed as a structure. That was the whole of
the coupling: a schematic API, a process, an is-it-running question and a stop-it call, for a
request tungsten's build queue already takes directly — walk to a placing position, aim with a live
raytrace, place through the same rate gate as everything else here, drained on the client tick.

Which block to place stays in altoclef, because it is altoclef's question: `toPlace` is a preference
list and `useThrowaways` opens it to whatever the settings call disposable. `forceEquipItem` answers
it and puts the item in the hotbar, which is where the queue equips from.

`ClearRegionTask` moved onto `DestroyBlockTask`, one cell at a time, and picked up a fix on the way.
Its `isFinished` had two bugs of one shape: the loops excluded the far plane, and collapsed to zero
iterations on any axis where `from == to` — so a flat or single-column region reported "finished"
without checking a single block. The tick and the finish test now share **one** box walk, so they
cannot disagree about which cells are in the box.

With the only starter gone, `Nav.isBuilding()` could only answer false and `Nav.stopBuilding()` could
only do nothing. Both are deleted, with their two remaining call sites and a `getBuilderProcess()`
fetch in `EnterNetherPortalTask` that had no `.onLostControl()` on the end of it. **Nothing in
altoclef touches `BuilderProcess` any more.** Imports of `baritone.*`: **18 → 14**.

### Two corrections to what the last release said about G-0

**"All 18 remaining are LIVE code" was wrong.** Two of the four builder imports were in classes
nothing calls — `PlaceBlockSchematic` and `ClearRegionTask`, checked across every module, the
commands, the py4j surface and the MCP tool list. The first is deleted. The second is kept and
ported, because "clear this box" is a lever an agent will want and it costs three lines once the box
walk exists — but it is marked at the site as having no caller, so its first caller should expect to
be its first test.

**The import count understates the coupling.** The goal API is used *fully qualified* at about ten
more sites (`GoalBlock`, `GoalXZ`, `GoalNear`, `GoalGetToBlock`, `GoalYLevel`, `GoalTwoBlocks`,
`GoalComposite`), and so are the schematic formats and `MovementHelper.isLava`. An import count is a
proxy for coupling and this one reads low. The remaining work is bigger than 14 lines suggests.

Left alone deliberately: `EnterNetherPortalTask` cancels four processes and two of the calls only
*fetch* the process and drop it. Adding the missing `.onLostControl()` changes portal-entry
behaviour rather than tidying it, so it gets its own pass and its own test.

**Tested:** the craft suite on the deployed jar — 0 gate failures, 11/12 ok, the one non-pass an
INVALID from a starved host. `craft_table` at 28.0 fps is the direct `PlaceBlockTask` path;
`mine_stone`, `chop_tree` and `chop_canopy` cover `DestroyBlockTask`, whose `stopBuilding()` call was
removed.

## The bench learned to say which measurements may be compared

Three holes, all of the same family: the suite recorded numbers that looked comparable and were not.

**The frame rate is on every SUMMARY line now.** The floor at 14 fps is absolute; comparability is
relative. A course that ran at 29 and then runs at 17 is above the floor twice over and is still not
the same measurement — half the frames means half the aim corrections, and aim is delivered per
frame. The summary is the part that gets quoted into before/after tables, and it omitted the one
number that decides whether two rows may be compared at all. It earned itself immediately: the craft
sweep reads 28–30 for ten courses and then 13.2 and 10.0 for the last two, and the pvp sweep shows
`chase_terrain` passing at **17.7** against everything else at 29.

**A repeat of the same course is compared with its own best.** A drop past a quarter is marked
`[fps drift 29.3 → 17.1 - not comparable]` and spends a rebuild from the existing capped budget.
Same course only: a 5×5 platform and a 90 s chase over terrain do not owe each other a frame rate.
The branch was **forced and watched** before being trusted — ratio set above 1.0 for one `--repeat 2`
run — because the two guards beside it each had a hole visible only on the path that fires, and one
of them crashed the eight-run series it existed to protect.

**A build landing mid-series is now a witness, not an operator rule.** A client refresh deploys
whatever sits in `build/libs` when it fires, and the starvation and drift guards made refreshes
common and mid-series. The suite fingerprints the jar that `deploy_jar.sh` would copy — at start and
before every run — and marks every run after a change with `jar_changed`. Detection, not prevention:
the refresh still deploys the new artefact, and holding the suite's own copy is a bigger change than
this earns.

## Both halves of a duel were being measured, and only one was being read

Every mutual duel is judged on kills minus deaths. About 27 exchanges decide a run, so that margin
carries a standard deviation near **2.7** — six healthy `narrow_bridge_duel` runs read
`+3, −2, −3, +1, −4, +1`. At n=6 the resolution is about ±2.2, and every mechanism proposed for that
course has been smaller than its own spread. Three claims were made and withdrawn this session on
exactly that arithmetic.

The three duels are not equally able to measure anything, and it is the arena rather than the code:

| course | sd of `kills − deaths` | runs per arm to see a 1-point shift |
|---|---|---|
| `melee_basic` (open field) | **0.75** | ~6 |
| `edge_duel` (5×5 platform) | **2.4** | ~46 |
| `narrow_bridge_duel` (1-wide bridge) | **2.7** | ~57 |

So `DamageWatch` is now read on **both** fighters. It counts from the client tick, so it had been
running on the opponent all along — both duellists run this mod — and no probe had ever asked. A run
yields ~50 blows a side against ~13 deaths. Read as a **ratio between the sides**, never as a total:
the class's own javadoc records that its damage does not reconcile with deaths × 20, and a shared
undercount cancels in a ratio while it does not in a sum. That is the fourth time this session the
instrument turned out to be already in the repo.

## Where the pvp suite actually stands

Full sweep on the shipped combat build: **10/12, two gate failures.**

`melee_basic` PASS (5:5), `narrow_bridge_duel` PASS (14:13), `chase_flat`, `chase_terrain`,
`ranged_moving`, `bridge_assault`, `bridge_assault_defended`, `slab_hole` PASS.

`allround` FAIL, and **the criterion it fails is one the course cannot pass**: `bot deaths <= 0`
over 120 s of mutual combat, against an opponent that punks back the whole time, with an iron sword,
no armour anywhere in the suite and four blows to a kill. Everything the course was built to test
passes — ranged hits 4 and 5, kills 4 and 6, freezes 0, and the chase measured clean again
(`steer 406/406`, `los 407/407/0/0`). That is the mirror of a check that cannot fail, and worth as
little. It gets re-gated on what allround is actually for, not relaxed by a number.

`edge_duel` FAIL (7:10, and 7:9 on the retry). Both at 29 fps, both with `self=0 knockback=0`, so
the void is not doing it. **No claim is made from that**: this course's spread is 2.4 and n=2 gives a
standard error of 1.7. Three claims died of exactly this arithmetic already; a series at n≥6 an arm
is running, and it is the same series that gives the course its first honest baseline — the victim
pin that made it asymmetric landed yesterday, so nothing measured before that compares.

### What that series is really asking

Every pvp duel pins the **victim** to `combatReachControl=false` and punks both sides with the same
controller and the same kit. So each duel is already a controlled comparison of that one setting,
inside a single run, at identical fps — the bot carries it, the opponent does not.

Worth knowing while reading it: on `melee_basic`, the sharpest of the three, the bot's margin with
the setting is `0, −1, 0, 0, +1, −1` plus this sweep's 5:5. n=7, median **0**, so on the open field
`combatReachControl` is worth approximately nothing — not the win its javadoc claims, and not a loss
either. And the evidence that turned it on does not survive re-reading: it cites `allround`,
"deaths 1 and 1 with it against 2 and 3 without", n=2 an arm, on the course that was proven to be
ending at ~20 s of its 120 s.
