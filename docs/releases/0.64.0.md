# 0.64.0 — a ledge, a pool and a ladder

Obstacle course (`deploy/runner/run_suite.py nav`): **6/10 → 9/10** on single
runs. `nav_wall2` (two-block ledge), `nav_water` (pool) and
`nav_ladder` (ladder to a shelf) pass for the first time.

Honesty caveat: 9/10 is PER-COURSE runs. A single back-to-back series of all ten courses
on this bench sags in fps toward the end and gives a worse result; a course whose ONLY
failures are load-sensitive checks at low fps is now marked `INVALID`, not a
"regression". That is a bench problem, not a bot problem, and it is recorded.

| course | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `nav_wall2` | never passed | **PASS**, 6.3 s (3/3 reach the goal) |
| `nav_water` | never passed | **PASS**, 6.3-9.5 s, 4/4 |
| `nav_ladder` | never passed | **PASS**, 5.7-6.6 s, 5/5 |
| `nav_gaps`, `nav_break`, `nav_steep`, `nav_flat`, `nav_staircase`, `nav_descend` | PASS | PASS (no regression) |

## What was fixed: a chain of three, each link hiding behind the previous one

This is exactly the kind of case `docs/NAVIGATION.md` was set up for: three fully
written, working mechanisms in a row — and none of them could fire.

**1. The handoff to physics was starved.** Everything around it already worked
correctly: the climb was generated (`CLIMB EMITTED ... rise 2.00`), the route reached
the top of the ledge, the segment was cut exactly where it needed to be (`PLAN
complete=true flagged=1`) — and the handoff was still rejected **on every tick**. Cause:
there is **one** physics search engine in the system, and the `;goto` command was
running a second search on it in parallel — toward the final goal. The goal sits on top
of the ledge, which physics cannot climb, so the search could never finish: it burned
the full 20 s, restarted, and kept the engine busy forever (`pending=set pfActive=TRUE`).

**2. The first attempt to free the engine broke `nav_gaps`** and was reverted — which
produced the false conclusion that the two pipelines were propping each other up. They
are not. Rereading `GotoCommand.startWithRetry` in full showed: that edit exited the
function too early and, in the process, also skipped setting the **retry callback** a
few lines below — so after the very first `;goto` segment, nobody continued. If you skip
**only the search**, every course stays green.

**3. Once the engine was freed, the handoff fired** (`HANDOFF target=(12,-58,0)
rise=1.48 horiz=2.18`) — and physics was asked to climb 1.48 blocks, which no jump
covers. The goal **above you and nearly overhead is a wall, not a jump**: you can only
get up there by placing a block under yourself. `PillarTask` does exactly that (hold
center, jump, place in the air), it ticks from a client mixin and is exposed to py4j.
Navigation simply never called it. Now it does — at the handoff point.

Result in the log: `Pillaring up to y=-58`, and 4 of 4 attempts ran cleanly
(`Pillar done ... (placed 1)`, not a single "stuck", not a single "no block in hand").

## Water: three roots in one chain

**1. The search dropped any node without support.** The main loop pulled a node, asked
`supportTop`, and moved on when it was NaN. But water and ladder cells are unsupported
**by definition** — the `special()` generator created them, and the very next line
discarded them, never letting them expand once. So water and ladder routes came out
`complete=FALSE flagged=0`, no matter what moves were added to the set.

**2. Water entry was checked at the wrong level.** You **descend** into a pool: its
surface is usually one block below the bank. Water was checked at its own level and
above — but the cell next to the pool at foot level is AIR above the water. An ordinary
pool never got entered. While at it, the coordinate convention was verified rather than
taken from memory (`node.y` is the FEET cell): the diagnostic had claimed the opposite
and was wrong.

**3. The pool had no bottom.** Reading the arena builder wasn't enough — it took
tracing. The arena floor is one block thick over the void, and the pool is carved three
down: the result was a hanging cube of water with no floor and no walls. With the first
two fixes in, the bot swam to the middle and fell straight through: `(12.6,-64,-4.2)` —
below the bottom and outside the pool's bounds — and flew off to y=-169. Now the pool is
carved inside a solid block.

## Ladder: no executor could climb it, each for a different reason

**Physics** only emits its climb move if the agent is **already standing in the ladder
column** (`Node.java:133`, Manhattan distance ≤ 0.5). Standing on the ground next to it —
distance ~1.0 — and the move never fired. The ladder had been handed to physics on the
grounds that "only real simulation can hold onto a rung".

**The walker** advances the route point by **horizontal** distance. For a vertical
column that is zero, so the condition is true from the very first tick: the whole climb
got swallowed in one tick, without moving up by a millimeter, after which the walker
reported "arrived".

Now the walker owns the ladder — the executor that actually knows how. Arrival on a
ladder is judged by **verticality**, and it aims at the block the ladder is attached to:
in vanilla, climb speed comes precisely from pressing into the ladder. Both fixes are
gated behind a "we are on a ladder" condition, so nothing changed off ladders.

Also removed: one more instance of an already-known trap — the slime scan called
`passableAt` with an absolute height of `0.1` — "does the body fit at height 0.1", open
sky, always yes — and so it searched for slime through a solid floor.

## The water course no longer also tests the limit jump

The start platform ended at x=6, the pool began at x=11 — four cells of void between
them, which the bot cleared with a jump right at the engine's limit. It made it in a
single run, not in a series. A course that claims "swim across" should not be gated on
edge-case parkour.

## Bench: clips came out empty because the bot got faster than the recording

Every clip came out with a 28-byte header. ffmpeg itself was fine — a manual
three-second capture produced 313 KB. Recording uses a **fragmented** mp4, a fragment
flushes on a keyframe, and the default keyframe interval is 250 frames, i.e. ~17 seconds
at 15 fps. Courses now finish in 6 seconds, and the capture always stopped before the
first fragment. One keyframe per second — clips became 1.3-5.8 MB.

## Bench: an overloaded machine no longer masquerades as a regression

The bench holds the server, the client and sometimes the build on one machine, and by
the end of a long series it sags in fps on its own.

This one is also a fix for my own mistake. A full run reported "7 regressions",
including `nav_flat`, green a minute earlier. I attributed it to machine overload and
built a guard around that. **The diagnosis was wrong.** The real cause: the freeze
detector counted it as a violation for the bot to be STANDING ON A GOAL ALREADY REACHED
— the exception for this case only worked on combat courses, where the distance to the
victim gets filled in. Fixed at the cause, and the guard's comment was rewritten so it
does not pass itself off as an explanation for that case.

Now a course whose **only** failures are load-sensitive checks at low fps is marked
`INVALID` with a direct instruction to re-run, and does not count. An unreached goal is
never forgiven. Exit code 2 = "not a regression, but not proof either".

## What this changes in substance

**The route now has a single owner.** While the navigator is driving, `;goto` no longer
searches for its own path to the goal: the navigator owns the route and only asks
physics for the sections it cannot walk itself. Previously two pipelines pressed the
same keys and fought over the single search engine.

## Known issues

- One of three `nav_wall2` runs took 16.5 s and hit one freeze-detect. The pillar logs
  are clean, so the snag is **before** the handoff. Not "fixed" blindly — recorded as an
  observation.
- `nav_slime` remains red — a jump puzzle (drop 8 blocks onto slime, bounce onto a ledge).
- A back-to-back series of all ten courses sags the bench in fps; per-course runs are
  what should be trusted for now.
- Block placement is still **not priced into** the search: a climb costs the same
  whether or not it needs a block placed under it.
- After death the bot does not resume `;goto` — in the water run with a fall into the
  void it respawned and stood still until the end. Separate item.
