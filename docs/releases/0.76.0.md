## The bot kept backing out of fights to use a bow it did not have

`CombatController` broke contact whenever the bot dropped below half a bar and the opponent was
outside sword reach. The comment above that branch justifies it plainly: out past reach the bow
becomes the weapon. Nothing in the branch ever asked whether there was a bow.

On a sword-only kit the retreat buys nothing and spends everything. The bot walks out of range,
deals no damage, cannot heal — the bench turns natural regeneration off and the kit carries no
food — and hands the initiative over for the rest of a life that the low-health latch has already
claimed. The controller had already measured where those ticks went: **47% of every close-quarters
tick** (180 of 385) was going into this retreat.

Three courses line up by the **cost** of retreating, which is the part the earlier reading of this
got wrong — it is not how much room there is to retreat, it is what retreating costs:

| course | arena | cost of the retreat | before |
|---|---|---|---|
| `edge_duel` | 5×5 platform | cannot retreat at all | green the whole time |
| `melee_basic` | open field | loses tempo | coin flip, 2 PASS in 6 |
| `narrow_bridge_duel` | 1-block bridge | **it is how you fall off** | worst: −3 and −6 |

The fix asks `WeaponSelector.hasRangedOption` first: a launcher in hotbar or offhand, and
ammunition anywhere in the inventory — the launcher has to be held to be fired, but vanilla finds
arrows wherever they are, and asking a narrower question would have made the answer depend on
where a `/give` happened to land.

### ⛔ The "regression" this section reports was a MIRROR, and it did not happen

Read the next section knowing this: **`edge_duel` gives the opponent the same build as the bot.**
`Scenario.victim_settings` defaults to `{}` and that course never overrode it, so a symmetric engine
change goes to both fighters and cancels — expected margin 0 by construction, pass rate a coin flip
with ties passing. `melee_basic` and `narrow_bridge_duel` pin the victim to
`combatReachControl=false` for exactly this reason and `TungstenConfig`'s javadoc says so outright.
`edge_duel` never got the pin.

So the n=8 numbers below are a coin, the 4/4 baseline was luck (~2% at that rate — unlikely, not
impossible), and the amendment they prompted was made in response to something the course cannot
measure. The amendment still stands on its own mechanism, and whether it is kept is decided by
`melee_basic`, which can discriminate. The course now carries the pin, so it can serve as a guard in
future.

The section is kept rather than deleted because the sequence is the lesson: a guard was chosen
without checking whether it could see the thing it was guarding, the doctrine that would have caught
it was quoted the same day from another file, and eight careful runs produced a confident and empty
conclusion.

### The first version of that fix was too wide — measured on a course that could not see it

The branch did **two** things and only one of them was wrong. `kite()` sets `forward = false`, and
*then* steps back only when `dirSafe(back)`. On `edge_duel` — a 5×5 platform over void —
`dirSafe(back)` is false almost everywhere near the rim, so there was never a retreat there at all:
only a bot that stopped walking into a fight it was losing. Deleting the branch whole deleted that
hold as well.

It cost the course. `edge_duel` at n=8 on a healthy stand (27.8–29.5 fps): `+1, −1, +1, −3, −4, +1,
−2, −5` — median **−1.5**, three passes in eight, against a course that closed **4/4** with the
branch in place.

Two things are worth recording about how that was caught rather than shipped. The guard's **first
run read 9:8 PASS** — a single-run regression check, which is what had originally been queued, would
have certified it. And the assumption that made a single run look sufficient — *"edge_duel is
unaffected, the branch barely fires on a 5×5"* — was registered in writing and then refuted by its
own witness: the branch fires 33–59 ticks a run there, more than on the open field.

So the branch was made to **hold without withdrawing**: below half health, out of reach, nothing to
shoot with → stop closing, do not step back. Implemented as a flag rather than `kite()`'s early
return, because that return also skips the circle-strafe, the crit hop and the trigger.

**And then it was measured and removed.** On `melee_basic` — the course that CAN discriminate,
because its victim is pinned — six healthy runs (26.4–29.4 fps) came back `−1, −2, −2, −2, −1, 0`:
median **−1.5**, one pass in six, against the plain fix's median 0 and four passes in six. The hold
costs the bot exactly the forward pressure its gain was made of. A seventh run returned +2 and was
the only one taken at 10 fps; today's starvation guard marked it and it is excluded, which is the
fourth time in this session that a starved run flattered the bot.

So the shipped behaviour is the plain fix: refuse the retreat, keep the advance. The amendment is
recorded here rather than quietly dropped, because it was written in answer to a regression that the
mirror course could not actually have shown — and both halves of that mistake are worth carrying
forward.

### What it measured

⚠ **The `melee_basic` figures in this section were taken on the FIRST, too-wide version** — the one
that deleted the hold along with the retreat. They are kept because they are what established the
defect was real, but they do not describe the build being shipped. Both courses are being
re-measured at n=8 on the amended code, and these numbers will be replaced by those.

`lowHp`, the counter for ticks spent in that retreat, reads **0** on every bowless run now. That
one is exact: it does not need eight runs to be believed.

`melee_basic`, the six runs taken at a healthy frame rate (27.9–29.6 fps, against a baseline at
29.2 and 28.5): margins `0, −1, 0, 0, +1, −1` — median **0**, verdicts **4 PASS / 2 FAIL**. The
baseline was median −1, verdicts 2 PASS / 6. Two further runs came in at 10 fps and are excluded;
both were passes, so the exclusion costs this claim rather than helping it.

Being honest about that: the median moved by one and the range is two wide, so **the margin alone
does not prove anything** by this repo's own variance rule. What carries it is three readings
agreeing — the exact mechanism counter, a won exchange (+1) that appears nowhere in the entire
baseline, and the pass rate.

`narrow_bridge_duel` is **not reported here**, and it took two attempts to fail to report it. The
first series returned +1, +4, +1, +2, −3 against a baseline of −3 and −6, which looked decisive and
was not: every run was taken at 10.0 fps while the baseline had been recorded at 29.3 and 18.1. The
re-measurement on fresh clients reached five of its eight runs and was then killed by the starved-
stand guard itself (below), leaving +3, −2, −3, +1 at a healthy frame rate and one more at 9.9 that
does not count. The full sweep then added −4 (11:15 at 28.8 fps), so the healthy series reads
`+3, −2, −3, +1, −4` — median **−2** against a baseline median of −4.5, inside the spread, and the
course still fails.

So: no claim. The course is unresolved, not improved, and the number that would have been quotable
was the contaminated one.

Worth noting against the mechanism story, since it was wrong twice here: the deaths on that bridge
are **blows, not falls**. `self=0` and `knockback=1–2` against 16–17 deaths per run, before the fix
and after. And the fix removes *more* there than on `melee_basic` — 136–187 ticks a run against 40 —
while showing less effect, which is the opposite of what "the retreat is what costs the trade"
predicts. Either the spread is drowning it or the cause on that course is something else.

### The amount removed does not predict the outcome

`lowHpDeclined` counts the ticks the predicate refuses — the retreat that would have fired and no
longer does. Measured per course, on the same jar:

| course | ticks removed per run | what happened to the exchange |
|---|---|---|
| `melee_basic` | 40 | improved |
| `edge_duel` | 40–57 | was green, still green |
| `narrow_bridge_duel` | 136–187 | unchanged |

The ordering is not the ordering of the effect, on any of the three. So the fix reliably removes
the behaviour it targets, and something else decides who wins the trade. Worth saying plainly,
because the tempting version of this release note — "removed a defect, courses improved" — would
imply a dose-response that the numbers do not show.

It also killed an assumption of mine that had been registered in writing before the run: *"edge_duel
is unaffected, the branch barely fires on a 5x5"*. It fires 40–57 ticks a run there, more than on
the open field. The counter that refuted it had been added to that probe an hour earlier precisely
because the claim was an assumption and had been labelled one.

### Two corrections to things that were already written down

**`hurtBackingOff` counts a position, not a key.** It increments on "hurt and closer than
backOffAt", and has never looked at whether the back key was pressed. Since retreating under fire
was disabled, a high reading now means the opposite of what the name suggests: the bot is standing
in the trade. Misread exactly that way during this work, off `115/45/65`, which would have aimed a
fix at a mechanism already fixed in the same file.

**Every "the bot loses a symmetric duel" figure from before this release is suspect.** The duel
victim is pinned to `combatReachControl=false`, so it never entered that block and never carried
the retreat. The bot did. The duels were not symmetric — one side had a handicap — which includes
the `4:4, 4:6, 4:4, 3:4` series and the "distance tuning is exhausted" verdict above it.

### Bench

The `narrow_bridge` probe asked whether the fighters ever came within 3.0 blocks and reported
"they never met" on a run with **twelve kills**. The 3.0 was guessed; the blows say the bot kills
at 4.25 mean and 5.35 max. It now reads the band from the mod, like the `bow_flee` probe does —
two copies of a distance is how that constant went wrong twice already.

The frame-rate auto-refresh proved itself in both directions in one sweep: `chase_terrain` went
9.0 fps → PASS on fresh containers, and `bow_flee_hard` went 10.0 fps → FAIL. A repair path that
only ever turns INVALID into green cannot be told apart from laundering.

**And then it turned out to have a hole big enough to lose a measurement through.** The refresh
only fired on INVALID, and INVALID needs a load-sensitive criterion to *fail* — so a course that
happened to *pass* while starved sailed through, the stand was never refreshed, and every later
run in the series inherited the degradation. That is how five runs of a before/after comparison
got spent comparing two different stands while the summary printed five clean passes. `melee_basic`
in the same series held 27.9–29.6 for six runs and then fell to 9.9, so the wear arrives
mid-series, exactly when a comparison is least able to notice it.

Any run under the floor is now marked on the spot, labelled `[starved — not comparable]` in the
SUMMARY where numbers actually get quoted, and triggers a client refresh before the next run. The
verdict itself still stands — it passed, and that is real — it just cannot be set against a
healthy baseline.

**That guard then had a hole of its own, in the same place twice.** It inspected the FIRST attempt,
while the flake retry replaces the result afterwards — so a retry's frame rate was never looked at,
and a retry is precisely where a "passed on the second go" verdict comes from. Caught in the sweep
that was meant to certify this release: `narrow_bridge_duel` failed 11:15 at 28.8 fps, retried at
9.9, came back 17:11, and a clean PASS went into the summary. On that course it matters more than
elsewhere, because starvation there flatters the bot systematically — the discarded 10 fps series
read as the best result of the session, and now the best single run of the next one was again the
only starved one. The recorded verdict is checked too now, and the rebuilds are capped at two per
invocation so a repeat-8 series cannot spend half an hour rebuilding a stand that foreign load is
slowing.
