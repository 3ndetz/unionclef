# 0.85.0 — the bench stops throwing away half of every measurement

Nothing in this release makes the bot fight better. It makes finding out whether a change helped
about twice as cheap, which is what had been blocking every combat hypothesis for weeks.

## The defect

Two guards in the test harness were discarding healthy runs, and a third printed the wrong reason
for all of them.

- `DamageWatch` records, for every drop in the fighter's health, the distance to the closest OTHER
  living entity. Once the target dies, that is whatever else the world holds — on the bench, the
  second tester client parked across the map. So fall damage taken after a kill entered the ledger
  as a hit from ~63 blocks.
- The suite's stand-sanity guard read the MAX of those distances and voided the whole run, on the
  reasoning that a hit from that far means the arena has emptied. One post-kill scratch was enough.
- Whatever fired, the summary printed the frame-rate explanation. One run was discarded with
  `client at 28.0 fps, below the 14.0 floor` — a sentence that contradicts itself.

Measured cost: 7 of 14 runs invalid in one series, then 6 of 13, then 10 of 16.

## The fix

- Health drops with no living entity within 32 blocks are no longer attributed to anything. A bow
  carries about 16 blocks and melee 4.5, so past that there was no attacker. Dropped samples are
  counted as `dwNoBlame` and printed, rather than vanishing.
- The stand guard reads the mean instead of the max, so one outlier cannot cost a run. A genuinely
  emptied arena still trips it, because then every hit lands far.
- Each guard records why it fired and the summary prints that reason.

After deploying: 2 invalid of 11 runs. A twelve-run arm now costs about fifteen launches instead of
twenty-four.

## Also in this release

- `dealt` and `swingHits` — damage taken off the target, and how much of it followed one of our own
  swings within a couple of ticks. The first is bounded by the target's health and says less than it
  looks like it does; the javadoc says so at the field.
- The frame rate is recorded as bimodal (9-10 fps or 28.5-29, nothing between), which makes it a
  switch rather than a render ceiling. The obvious suspect — the second client — was measured and
  does not carry the effect. Written up at the floor for whoever picks it up.

## Known state

`mob_skeleton` is still red. It passed twice during this work, on the build that had been failing,
and then 0 of 9 valid runs passed on the next series — that was a draw from the tail, not a fix, and
the course notes say so next to the passing runs.
