# 0.44.0

Terrain climbing got a lot more reliable. The bot no longer spins in circles at the foot
of a staircase.

## Face-before-move (the climb spin fix)

White-boxing a failing climb (per-tick walker log: waypoint, yaw vs target yaw, velocity)
turned up the real cause of the flaky staircase climbing. The walker pressed **forward
every tick regardless of which way it faced**. While the humanized WindMouse camera was
still turning toward the next waypoint, the bot was already walking — in the wrong
direction. That shifted the waypoint's bearing, which moved the aim target, which the
camera then chased… a feedback loop. The trace showed the yaw sweeping ~680° and the bot
**spiralling in a circle** instead of climbing. Sometimes it converged before the loop
destabilised (climb succeeded); sometimes it locked into the spin (failed) — that was the
~40% flakiness.

The fix is the standard pathing rule: **turn to face the waypoint first, then walk** —
but only while on the ground. Movement is gated on the yaw being within 45° of the target
so the bot pivots in place to line up, then goes straight. Crucially the gate is lifted
while **airborne**: a gap jump or slime bounce sets its direction at take-off, and cutting
forward mid-arc would kill the momentum and drop the bot short. So: face-before-move on the
ground, keep the momentum in the air.

Measured on the stand (`diag_climb_multi` / `slime_test`, 8 fresh runs each):

- 3-wide staircase (realistic terrain climb): **6/8 → 7–8/8**.
- Parkour gaps (running jumps, course B): **4/8 → 8/8**.
- Slime drop-bounce + flat: PASS (unaffected).

A 1-block-wide staircase is still flaky near the very top (it demands near-perfect lateral
precision on a single-block ledge that real terrain doesn't) — tracked, not shipped as
fixed.

Also lands the diagnostic tooling used to find this: a gated per-tick walker log
(`setWalkerDebug`) and multi-run climb harness with course/width parameters.
